Madison Square Park: which stated principle walks like the person did?

One person crossed the park from the northwest corner to the southern tip. Three simulated walkers cross it too: one takes the shortest route, one sticks to the park's edges, one stays under trees. The page scores how close each walker stays to the person, and marks where the person left the shortest route, which is where some principle may have been at play.

How the walkers choose, and what this can't see

Shortest: the shortest way along the paths. Edge: first minimizes the feet it walks farther than the edge distance from the boundary, then the length. Shade: first minimizes the feet it walks outside any tree's canopy, then the length. Each is a full preference for one principle, with no mixing.

Not seen: other people and how the person dodged them, real tracked paths, benches, sun angle and time of day (shade is fixed, every Full tree always casts it), seasons, leaf cover, and why the person chose their route. The paths come from a map of the park and may miss some. The 276 tree count is the city's records and may be high.
